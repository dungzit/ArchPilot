## 1. What to learn from the references

### SystemsArchitect.io

Useful framework elements:

- 6R lifecycle: Request, Requirements, Review, Resolve, Realize, Reassess.
- 6U user and business discovery: pitch, roles, qualities, UI, UX/screens, projections.
- Platform workflow: application, data, storage, networking, security, DevOps.
- UCROPS: Usability, Cost, Reliability, Operations, Performance, Security.
- Architecture pattern checklist and design funnel.
- Data workflow: use cases, access patterns, API design, and data model.

ArchPilot adopts these as structured workbench modules with evidence, scoring, sizing, deployment, financial value, and exports.

### SysDesAi

Useful product patterns:

- Step-by-step AI architect that asks clarifying questions.
- Architecture diagrams, data models, sequence flows, and capacity estimates as connected outputs.
- Interactive chat at each design stage, with copilot and autopilot modes.
- Gallery of complete architecture walkthroughs that can be explored and forked.
- Community discussion and architect profiles.
- Learning academy with lessons, tests, and certificates.
- Curated architecture news with summaries focused on decisions and trade-offs.

ArchPilot should borrow the interaction patterns, not copy content or branding. Its differentiation is the personal decision workbench, target-neutral deployment, sizing evidence, UCROPS review, and Investment & Technology Value.

## 3. AI Design Copilot contract

When implemented, AI must operate as a guided assistant:

1. Ask for missing context and NFRs.
2. Propose options, not a single unquestionable answer.
3. Generate structured artifacts: requirements, diagrams, sequence flows, sizing inputs, ADR draft, and review questions.
4. Cite internal or public sources.
5. Mark inference versus verified fact.
6. Show confidence and unresolved assumptions.
7. Require architect confirmation before changing a decision.
8. Never provision production directly.

## 4. Design gallery contract

A reusable design must contain:

- Problem statement.
- User and business context.
- Deployment target.
- Components and data flows.
- UCROPS scorecard.
- Sizing assumptions.
- Cost/TCO assumptions.
- Failure modes and security controls.
- Trade-offs and rejected alternatives.
- Source citations.
- Version, owner, applicability, and review date.

Forking a design creates a new revision with the source design ID; it must not mutate the original.

## 5. Product boundary

ArchPilot is not trying to become a generic social network, course platform, or news reader. Community and learning are later adoption features. The core product remains a decision workbench where knowledge is converted into reviewed architecture and value outcomes.
